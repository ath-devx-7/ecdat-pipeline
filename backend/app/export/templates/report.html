7. Methodology and citations

Discovery. Six collectors observe cryptography and record what they saw in the artefact's own words: certificates and key-file metadata, configuration files, source code through Semgrep, ELF binaries through their dynamic sections, live TLS handshakes against the declared probe targets only, and imported CycloneDX inventories. Private key material is never parsed. No collector makes a network call other than the probe of a host the user named.

Identity. Every spelling of an algorithm is resolved to one canonical family and OID through a cited alias table, so one algorithm is counted once however many tools spelled it. Findings carry a source layer — live, artifact, config, source — ordered by closeness to execution.

Verdicts. A lookup against a versioned, read-only policy pack ({{ policy.version }}). Every entry carries a citation to a published standard, reproduced beside the verdict in this report. No entry matching yields unknown. Broken-now and quantum-vulnerable are independent: AES and SHA-256 are quantum-safe, RSA-4096 is quantum-vulnerable and secure today, MD5 is broken today and irrelevant to quantum.

Drift. A configuration's declared protocol floor, ceiling or list is compared per probed service against the versions the handshake accepted or refused. A divergence is reported without classifying it as a misconfiguration or an exception.

Risk. Mosca's inequality, (X + Y) − Z > 0, where X is the data lifetime supplied for this scan, Y the migration duration and Z the years until a cryptographically relevant quantum computer. It is applied only to confidentiality primitives — key exchange and ciphers — because harvest-now-decrypt-later needs something recorded now that becomes readable later. Signatures and hashes have no harvest step, receive no urgency, and are placed in wave 3. Every input is stored on the finding so any wave can be reconstructed.

Recommendations. Matched on primitive plus family against the pack's migration targets; the parameter set follows the data lifetime; each prerequisite is tested against what the collectors observed on the asset; the hybrid preference is read from the pack. A target whose prerequisites are unmet is reported as blocked with the chain, and a finding no rule covers is reported as unknown rather than given a generic target.
